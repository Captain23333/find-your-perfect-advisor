# Research Proposal gates

Run these gates in order. `PASS` means the current RP meets the stated gate; it
does not mean admission, funding, or research success is likely.

## Gate 1 — Official requirement fit

- Exact program, degree, intake, purpose, deadline, length, format, language,
  sections, and citation style are sourced or explicitly unresolved.
- Advertised-project constraints and required methods are preserved.
- The proposal is not generic across programs with incompatible requirements.

Fail action: produce a concept note or requirement-gap list, not a falsely
final RP.

## Gate 2 — Question and literature grounding

- One main question is specific enough to select relevant literature.
- Central claims have inspected sources and correct evidence levels.
- The review compares studies and names consensus, contradiction, or boundary.
- The gap passes the four-question test and is not an absence inferred from an
  incomplete search.
- Closest work is addressed directly rather than hidden.

Fail action: narrow the question, expand the decisive search, or relabel the gap
as a hypothesis requiring verification.

## Gate 3 — Question–method alignment

- Each question/objective maps to data, access/sampling, method, analysis,
  validity limits, and ethics.
- Methods can observe or test the claimed phenomenon.
- Measures and outcomes are defined at proposal-appropriate detail.
- Alternative explanations and the strongest design limitation are visible.

Fail action: revise the question or method; do not add methodological jargon.

## Gate 4 — Feasibility and ethics

- Data, participants, facilities, equipment, compute, language skills, and
  supervision are available, plausibly obtainable, or explicitly assumptions.
- Recruitment/access and analysis fit the degree timeline.
- Ethics, consent, privacy, risk, and data handling are addressed where relevant.
- High-risk assumptions have verification actions and fallback routes.
- Timeline includes literature, approvals, collection/build, analysis,
  validation, and writing rather than only broad years.

Fail action: narrow the design, add a staged pilot, or mark the project as a
discussion draft. Never present hypothetical access as secured.

## Gate 5 — Contribution and target fit

- Claimed contribution follows from the reviewed gap and proposed design.
- Expected findings remain hypotheses, not results.
- Advisor/lab fit names specific expertise/resources and does not distort the
  applicant's core question.
- The proposal explains why this target helps while remaining a defensible
  research project independent of flattery.

Fail action: soften or split claims and remove unsupported impact language.

## Gate 6 — Citation and prose integrity

- Every in-text citation exists in the reference list and vice versa.
- Names, titles, years, DOIs/URLs, and substantive claims are verified.
- Quotations, if any, are exact and necessary.
- The draft obeys the word budget and official structure.
- The prose is clear enough for the applicant to defend orally and does not
  imitate a generic AI proposal.
- The strongest reviewer objection is answered or left as an explicit limit.

Fail action: do not label the draft final.
